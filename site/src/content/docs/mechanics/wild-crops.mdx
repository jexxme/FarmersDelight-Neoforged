Wild crops are the starting point of every Farmer's Delight farm. They look like tufts of leafy plants with a flower on top and break like tall grass. Breaking one gives the seed or the crop itself, so a single find is enough to start a field.

Seven wild plants generate: <Item id="wild_cabbages" />, <Item id="wild_onions" />, <Item id="wild_tomatoes" />, <Item id="wild_carrots" />, <Item id="wild_potatoes" />, <Item id="wild_beetroots" /> and <Item id="wild_rice" />. <Item id="sandy_shrub" plural /> come along with beach patches.

## How generation works

Each wild crop is added to biomes by a biome modifier that takes an allowed biome tag, an optional denied list and an optional range of base temperature <Src {...cite(wild.modifier)} />. The biome lists below are those filters already applied. Every patch also checks that its spot is in `#minecraft:is_overworld` <Src {...cite(wild.biomeTagFilter)} />.

For each chunk in an allowed biome:

1. The patch spawns with chance 1 in N (the rarity).
2. It picks a spot and makes a number of attempts around it ({wg('wild_cabbages').count} for most of them, {wg('wild_rice').count} for wild rice). Offsets follow a triangular ("trapezoid") spread, so plants cluster near the middle.
3. Each attempt first tries the crop, then the companion plant, each only on air above the right ground <Src {...cite(wild.inOrder)} />.

A single patch therefore holds many plants, and most chunks have none.

## Sandy shrub

<Item id="sandy_shrub" /> is not generated on its own: it only appears as the companion of beach cabbage and beetroot patches. It needs <Chips ids={blockTag('minecraft:sand')} compact /> below <Src {...cite(wild.shrubSand)} />. Bone meal always works on it and places a fresh patch of shrubs around it <Src {...cite(wild.shrubBonemeal)} />. A knife on a shrub can give <Item id="straw" /> ([Straw and rope](/mechanics/straw-and-rope/)).

## Spreading with bone meal

You can multiply wild crops before you have a field.

- **Wild cabbages, onions, tomatoes, carrots, potatoes, beetroots:** bone meal succeeds {pct(wild.bonemealChance.value, 0)} of the time <Src {...cite(wild.bonemealChance)} />. A success plants one copy on a free, valid spot a few blocks away (a short random walk). It does nothing once there are {wild.spreadLimit.value} or more of the same wild crop in the {wild.spreadArea.value.xz * 2 + 1}×{wild.spreadArea.value.y * 2 + 1}×{wild.spreadArea.value.xz * 2 + 1} area around it <Src {...cite(wild.spreadLimit)} />.
- **Wild rice:** bone meal succeeds {pct(wild.riceBonemeal.value, 0)} of the time and drops one <Item id="wild_rice" /> item instead of spreading <Src {...cite(wild.riceBonemeal)} />.

## Replanting

Wild crops can be placed back on <Chips ids={blockTag('minecraft:substrate_overworld')} compact /> or sand <Src {...cite(wild.placeOn)} />, so you can move them into a garden. Shears pick up the plant itself (see the drops above).

## Details

- Wild crops cannot be replaced by placing a block into them, unlike grass <Src {...cite('common/block/WildCropBlock.java:40-43')} />.
- They burn well: flammability {wild.flammable.value.flammability}, fire spread {wild.flammable.value.spread} <Src {...cite(wild.flammable)} />.
- The biome tags `farmersdelight:wild_*_whitelist` / `_blacklist` and `has_wild_*` still exist, but no biome modifier or code reads them. Editing them changes nothing; pack makers should override the biome modifiers instead.
- Some biome lists include oceans and cave biomes because the allowed tag is `#minecraft:is_overworld`. Patches sit on the surface heightmap and still need the right ground, so they rarely succeed there.
