A <Item id="cooking_pot" /> or a placed <Item id="skillet" /> only cooks while something hot is under it. Put it right
on top of a heat source, or put a <Item id="minecraft:hopper" /> in between. That's the whole rule, but a few details
decide whether your kitchen works.

## What heats a pot

The pot is heated when a heat source is **right below** it, or **two blocks below** with a hopper in between. Heat never
travels sideways, never through other blocks, and never more than two blocks. Campfires and the Stove only count while
they are lit.

The <Item id="stove" /> is the tidy choice: it is placed already lit and works at once. Put it out with a shovel or a
water bucket and the pot cools down (see [Stove](/mechanics/stove/)). Everything without a "yes" works all the time. Light
level doesn't matter.

## Trays

On fire, lava or a campfire, a pot or skillet stands on a small metal tray. The tray is
only looks: heating works the same with or without it. On {sources.filter((id) => !trays.includes(id)).map((id, i, a) => <>{i ? (i === a.length - 1 ? ' or ' : ', ') : ''}<Item id={id} /></>)} there is no tray, and a hanging pot never gets one.

## The hand-held skillet

Cooking with a <Item id="skillet" /> in your hand is more forgiving. You only need to be close to heat: any heat source in
the {reach}×{reach}×{reach} blocks around you, or being on fire yourself. Here an unlit campfire or Stove counts too, and
hoppers play no part. See [Skillet](/mechanics/skillet/).

## Good to know

- **Losing heat isn't a disaster.** Without heat, a pot or placed skillet loses progress, {timesAsFast(pot.decay.value, pot.cookStep.value)}
  as it built up, instead of starting over.
- **Only blocks count.** A burning mob or a lava bucket in your hand never heats a pot. The hand-held skillet is the one
  exception: it counts you as heat while you are on fire.
- **Modpacks can add heat sources.** If a pack adds its own, they follow the same rules, including "must be lit" for
  anything that can be lit.
